Vocational Rehabilitation, Step by Step

Vocational rehabilitation (VR) is a public program that helps people with disabilities prepare for, find, and keep a job. In North Carolina, VR is run by the Division of Employment and Independence for People with Disabilities, with more than 70 local offices. Services are planned around your own goals.

  1. Reach out to your local office. You can contact VR yourself, or a teacher, school counselor, doctor, or family member can help you make a referral.
  2. Find out if you are eligible. A VR counselor will talk with you and review information about your disability and how it affects work. Federal rules call for an eligibility decision within 60 days of applying.
  3. Build your Individualized Plan for Employment (IPE). You and your counselor write a plan together. It names your job goal and the services that will help you reach it. You have the right to make informed choices about your plan.
  4. Use the services in your plan. These can include career counseling, training or college support, assistive technology, job coaching, help finding a job, and supported employment.
  5. Start working and stay connected. VR keeps in touch after you start a job to make sure it is going well before your case is closed. You can come back for help later if things change.

Questions Families Often Ask a VR Counselor

  • What documents should we bring to the first meeting?
  • How will a job affect benefits such as SSI or Medicaid, and who can do benefits counseling?
  • Can VR help pay for training, college, or assistive technology?
  • What should we do if we disagree with a decision? (North Carolina's Client Assistance Program can help.)

Still in High School? Start with Pre-Employment Transition Services

Students with disabilities can begin working with VR before they graduate. Pre-Employment Transition Services (Pre-ETS) are available to North Carolina students ages 14 and older and include five core activities:

  • Job exploration counseling
  • Work-based learning, such as job shadowing and internships
  • Counseling on college and training options after high school
  • Workplace readiness training
  • Instruction in self-advocacy

Tip: ask your school to invite a VR counselor to IEP transition planning meetings so school and adult services are working from the same plan.
