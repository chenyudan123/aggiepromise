My name is Victoria Solomon, and I am a former research assistant for the AggiePromise Project. I graduated from North Carolina Agricultural and Technical State University in May 2024. I attended a university 12 hours away from my hometown of Peoria, Illinois. Sharing a little more about myself, some things I enjoy doing are playing Fortnite, hanging out with friends, and watching shows and movies. The AggiePromise Project focuses on young adults with disabilities, which also connects to my life. I write to you not only as a former member of the AggiePromise Project but also as an individual diagnosed with ADHD. I will share my experiences with ADHD, higher education, the workplace, my social life, and my future goals. Before getting into those experiences, I must first tell you about my experiences with ADHD before and after I experience trauma.

ADHD first showed up in middle school, when I displayed more hyperactive behaviors. I was a class clown but also maintained good grades. During sixth grade, I moved into a more advanced class, and my ADHD did not limit my academic performance. When I went to high school, the class clown in me died, and I became more focused on my academics. I graduated with a 3.8 GPA, although I only remember pushing myself particularly hard during my junior year because I was taking Precalculus. Throughout high school, I had good study habits, was never afraid to ask questions, and always strived for greatness. When I thought about going to college, I still expected greatness from myself.

Transitioning into higher education in 2020, my freshman year of college, I was still the same Victoria: good grades and a strict schedule. Then, my life became too busy, and I started experiencing mental health issues that impacted my habits. I had fallen into OCD thinking, and my college experience went from "obtain good grades, then have fun later" to "make an impact, have fun, then get good grades later." This shift happened in Spring 2022. In addition to this, the following summer, I experienced trauma that affected my mental health, worsened my ADHD symptoms, and brought my anxiety to an all-time high. Although that was my reality, pain pushed me toward purpose. My grades still were not as good as they were during my freshman year, but I walked into each room with identity. Besides the trauma, my college experience was great! I was popular, and although my trauma changed my social life, I still knew many people. However, access to me became harder. Now that I have healed from the situation, my ADHD symptoms are still more prevalent post-trauma, but I have also become a woman I never expected I would become, a woman chosen by God.

Transitioning into the real world, I started working as a Registered Behavior Technician (RBT), working with children with autism in the field of Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA). I have been in the field since September 2024, and I have discovered that I not only want to work with the ADHD population but with autistic individuals as well. My future goals are to become a school psychologist, work in academia, and obtain a PhD so I can work as a professor with a research team conducting research to benefit the ADHD and autistic communities. My research will focus on improving cognitive functioning, memory, attention, daily living skills, academic performance, and the transition into the workforce.

My goal is to make an impact on these communities by encouraging them to challenge stigmas, promoting success, and letting individuals know that their limitations do not define them and that they can achieve more than they could ever imagine.

Even though ADHD is a big part of my life, I do not allow it to define me, and neither should you. In life, we are dealt cards that we cannot control, but it is up to us to play with the cards we have and win, even with the limitations. I also recognize that ADHD is associated with distinct cognitive strengths, including creativity, divergent thinking, and innovative problem-solving. ADHD may be considered a disability, but I am not disabled, and neither are you.
