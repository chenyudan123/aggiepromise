My name is Ja’Quan Blanks, and I am a person living with cerebral palsy. Throughout my life, I have learned that having a disability does not mean having limitations on my dreams, aspirations, or purpose. Although my journey has presented challenges, it has also shaped my determination to succeed, advocate for others, and create meaningful change.

Growing up with a physical disability, I quickly learned that the world is not always designed with people like me in mind. From navigating physical environments to overcoming societal assumptions about what individuals with disabilities can accomplish, I have encountered barriers that required me to develop resilience, confidence, and a strong sense of self.

One of the most important lessons I have learned is that my disability is part of my identity, but it does not define the entirety of who I am. I am a scholar, researcher, leader, minister, advocate, and individual with dreams and aspirations just like anyone else.

Education has played a significant role in shaping my identity and opening doors to opportunities that once seemed beyond my reach.

My higher education journey at North Carolina Agricultural and Technical State University has helped me develop a passion for technology, leadership, research, and helping others overcome barriers.

In August 2025, I earned my Bachelor of Science in Computer Engineering from North Carolina A&T State University. In 2026, I completed my Master of Science in Information Technology with a 4.0 GPA.

Today, I am pursuing my Doctor of Philosophy in Leadership Studies at North Carolina A&T State University, focusing on interdisciplinary leadership.

While these accomplishments represent important milestones, the journey has not always been easy.

As a student with a physical disability, I have experienced accessibility challenges involving campus housing, transportation, buildings, and participation in university activities. There have been moments when I had to advocate for accommodations that would allow me to experience the same educational opportunities as my peers.

These experiences taught me that accessibility is about more than ramps, automatic doors, and physical accommodations. It is about dignity, belonging, independence, and ensuring that individuals with disabilities have an equal opportunity to participate in every aspect of university life.

I believe institutions of higher education have a responsibility not only to admit students with disabilities but also to create environments where those students can thrive academically, professionally, and socially.

My professional journey has provided opportunities to develop my leadership abilities while also revealing the challenges individuals with disabilities can face when seeking employment.

I have worked in information technology, university administration, and student support services. My professional experiences include serving as an IT Assistant Manager, an Executive Assistant in Career Services, a Graduate Assistant in the Graduate College, and currently as a Doctoral Graduate Assistant in the College of Education at North Carolina A&T State University.

These positions have allowed me to strengthen my skills in technology, communication, organizational leadership, research, and student engagement.

However, navigating the workforce as someone with a physical disability presents unique challenges. Transportation barriers, workplace accessibility, and assumptions about physical abilities can influence employment opportunities.

As someone who does not drive and relies on mobility equipment, I understand the importance of accessible transportation, flexible working arrangements, and inclusive hiring practices.

I believe employers should recognize the knowledge, qualifications, and potential that individuals with disabilities bring to the workplace rather than focusing solely on their physical limitations.

My experiences have strengthened my commitment to advocating for employment opportunities where individuals with disabilities are evaluated based on their abilities, skills, and contributions.

## Social Life, Independence, and Belonging

Living with a disability affects more than education and employment. It can also influence social relationships, independence, and participation in everyday activities.

Like many young adults, I enjoy connecting with others, participating in community activities, building meaningful relationships, and experiencing life beyond the classroom.

However, there have been times when inaccessible environments have made participation difficult. Whether attending university events, navigating crowded spaces, or participating in social activities, I have experienced situations where accessibility was not fully considered.

These experiences have helped me understand that inclusion involves more than simply inviting someone to participate. True inclusion requires intentionally creating environments where everyone can participate with dignity and independence.

I want to experience the same opportunities as my peers, including building friendships, developing relationships, enjoying recreational activities, pursuing a career, and eventually having a family of my own.

My disability does not diminish these desires. Instead, it reinforces my belief that individuals with disabilities deserve opportunities to experience every dimension of life.

## Faith, Purpose, and Personal Growth

My faith has been one of the most significant sources of strength throughout my journey.

As a minister and founder of Rescuing the Hostages Ministries, Inc., I believe that every individual has a purpose and that our circumstances do not determine the value of our lives.

Faith has helped me remain focused during challenging seasons and has encouraged me to view obstacles as opportunities for growth, advocacy, and transformation.

I have learned that resilience does not mean pretending that challenges do not exist. Rather, it means recognizing those challenges while continuing to pursue meaningful goals.

My experiences have also taught me the importance of using my voice to encourage others who may feel overlooked, excluded, or underestimated.

## My Future Goals and Research Interests

As I continue my doctoral journey, I am particularly interested in examining leadership approaches that support dignity, belonging, and identity formation among individuals with disabilities.

My research interests are deeply connected to my lived experiences.

I want to understand how educational institutions, employers, and community leaders can create environments where individuals with disabilities feel valued, respected, and empowered.

My long term goals include contributing to scholarly research, teaching in higher education, advancing disability advocacy, and influencing policies and leadership practices that promote accessibility and inclusion.

I also aspire to build a successful career, achieve financial stability, and eventually establish a family.

I want future generations of individuals with disabilities to recognize that their dreams are valid and that they deserve the opportunity to pursue those dreams without unnecessary barriers.

## My Experience With the AggiePromise Project

Being involved with the AggiePromise Project has been especially meaningful because it connects my lived experiences with research that seeks to improve opportunities for individuals with disabilities.

Contributing to research focused on the recruitment and retention of youth with disabilities has reinforced my belief that individuals with disabilities should not simply be subjects of research. We should also have opportunities to participate as researchers, collaborators, and leaders who help shape the questions being asked and the solutions being developed.

I am grateful to have contributed to the research article, [*The AggiePromise Project: Recruitment and Retention of Youth With Disabilities in Social Science Research*](https://doi.org/10.52017/001c.165065), published in the *Rehabilitation Counselors and Educators Journal* in 2026.

This accomplishment represents an important milestone in my development as a scholar and demonstrates the value of including individuals with lived experiences in academic research.

When I reflect on my journey, I recognize that my accomplishments have not come without challenges. There have been moments of frustration, uncertainty, and disappointment. However, those experiences have also strengthened my determination to create change.

I do not want my story to be viewed simply as an example of someone overcoming a disability. Instead, I want it to challenge society to reconsider the barriers that individuals with disabilities are expected to overcome in the first place.

My goal is not merely to succeed in a world that was not always designed for me. My goal is to help create a world where future generations of individuals with disabilities do not have to fight as hard to belong.

I hope my journey encourages others to pursue their education, advocate for their rights, embrace their identities, and recognize the value of their voices.

My story is still being written, and I believe the chapters ahead will provide even greater opportunities to lead, serve, and make a difference.

My disability is part of my story, but my purpose, potential, and future extend far beyond it.
